# 23.4 MTA-STS and SMTP TLS reporting

`python -m cyber_risk_intelligence.email_security mta-sts`
`python -m cyber_risk_intelligence.email_security summary mta-sts`

MTA-STS (RFC 8461) tells sending mail servers to deliver to a domain only over TLS with a valid
certificate. TLS reporting (RFC 8460) names where senders should report TLS delivery failures.
For each company domain the job makes:

1. a TXT lookup at `_mta-sts.<domain>` (`v=STSv1; id=...`), which announces a policy;
2. **only when that record exists** (present, invalid or multiple), one HTTPS GET of
   `https://mta-sts.<domain>/.well-known/mta-sts.txt`. Redirects are not followed (the RFC
   forbids it), the certificate must be valid, and at most 64 KB is read. The policy file is
   stored as evidence;
3. a TXT lookup at `_smtp._tls.<domain>` (`v=TLSRPTv1; rua=...`).

Both DNS answers are kept in `dns_security_records`, with their evidence, as for SPF and DMARC.
The report addresses are recorded, never contacted.

## `mta_sts_observations`

One row per domain per run. The DNS record, the policy fetch and the policy's content are kept
in separate columns, so a failure at one step is never read as "no MTA-STS".

| column | values |
|---|---|
| `dns_status` | `present`, `missing`, `invalid` (e.g. a bad `id=`), `multiple`, `lookup_failed` |
| `policy_retrieval` | `retrieved`, `not_found` (HTTP 404), `certificate_invalid` (expired, wrong host, untrusted), `request_failed` (timeout, other HTTP status including a redirect, over 64 KB), `not_attempted` (no record announced, or the lookup failed) |
| `policy_status` | `valid` or `invalid`; set only when the policy was retrieved |
| `mode`, `mx_patterns`, `max_age` | what the policy says |
| `problems` | why a record or policy is invalid |

A policy is valid when `version` is `STSv1`, `mode` is `enforce`, `testing` or `none`,
`enforce` and `testing` have at least one `mx` pattern, and `max_age` is a number up to one
year (31,557,600 seconds).

The database refuses a policy fetch without an announced record, a policy status without a
retrieved policy, and a retrieved policy without evidence.

## `tls_reporting_observations`

One row per domain per run: `status` (same values as `dns_status`), `record_text`, and
`report_uris`. A record with no `rua=`, or with an address that isn't `mailto:` or `https:`, is
`invalid`.

## Reading the results

- `mode = enforce` with a valid policy is the strong result. `testing` only reports failures,
  and `none` turns MTA-STS off.
- `certificate_invalid` or `not_found` while DNS announces a policy is a misconfiguration:
  senders can't use the policy.
- `lookup_failed` and `request_failed` mean unknown, not absent.
